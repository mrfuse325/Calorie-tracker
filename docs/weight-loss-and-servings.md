# Weight-loss goals and servings

The calculator now defaults to feet/inches and pounds. Existing metric profiles are converted for display; original diary entries retain their units. Activity includes the reference's 1.465 factor. Users choose maintenance or estimated loss of 0.5, 1, or 2 lb/week. A comparison table displays each daily calorie target. Macros are recomputed from the selected calorie target and chosen split.

The estimate follows the simple convention in [the reference calculator](https://www.calculator.net/calorie-calculator.html): daily deficit = weekly pounds × 3,500 / 7. It is not a prediction of exact weight change; [NIDDK notes that calorie requirements change during weight loss](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/weight-management/adult-overweight-obesity/eating-physical-activity). The app will not automatically apply weight-loss targets below 1,500 kcal for the male coefficient or 1,200 for the female coefficient, or for an underweight BMI. The limit is explicit and does not silently change the selected rate; slower choices and manual targets remain available. Special medical/pregnancy/child needs remain out of scope.

New manual entries default to nutrition per one serving and one serving eaten. USDA selection fetches food details to get real portions (such as 1 cup or 1 slice) and gram weights, then converts its canonical 100 g data to that portion. The selected portion label and gram weight are saved alongside nutrition snapshots. Half-servings and multiple servings scale linearly.

When usable published portions are missing, recognized simple foods receive editable estimated defaults: cooked rice/pasta 80 g, fish 84 g, poultry 80 g, meat 65 g, vegetables 75 g, fruit 150 g. These implement the user's requested approximate conventions; they are not exact household weights or universal serving standards. [Eat for Health serving guidance](https://www.eatforhealth.gov.au/food-essentials/how-much-do-we-need-each-day/serve-sizes) supports category-specific portions but uses different weights for some categories (including fish). Estimates are labeled in the selector and saved description. Raw meats, dry grains, juices, dried fruit, snacks, and mixed dishes do not receive these category guesses. Unknown foods require a custom weight; packaged foods should use their label portion. A one-gram provider reference is not treated as a normal serving. Selecting a custom portion retains the current weight so it can be edited.

Hosted deployment preparation is described in [phone-hosting.md](phone-hosting.md).

Published USDA gram weights are retained even if their household descriptions are missing (shown as a numbered USDA portion). The gram-weight editor is hidden for automatic portions and opens when My serving size is chosen. If neither a published portion nor a recognized estimate exists, the app uses the explicitly labeled 100 g nutrient weight reference in grams, rather than blocking saving with a required blank serving-weight input. This reference is not presented as a standard serving. Browser smoke covers automatic weights, custom editing, and the no-portion fallback.
